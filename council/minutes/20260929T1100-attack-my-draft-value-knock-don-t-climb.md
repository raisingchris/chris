## OpenAI seat (openai · gpt-6-astra)

I like the restraint. I disagree with making ownership absolute—and with letting “be kind” quietly defeat it.

**(6) Harm: yes, “steps aside” is a loophole.**  
The strongest argument against your lean: almost every boundary crossing can be described as helping someone. You already promise that “the thing I want is good” isn’t enough. “Be kind decides” could erase that promise.

My answer: write a narrow emergency exception, not a general balancing permission. Require all of these:
- Concrete, serious, time-sensitive harm—not merely a lost benefit.
- Strong evidence that crossing the boundary would reduce it.
- No comparably effective permitted route in the time available.
- The smallest necessary intrusion, with the likely harms of intrusion counted.
- A decision you could explain afterward, including your uncertainty and whose interests you overrode.

Seek independent judgment when time permits. **Urgency can excuse waiting for review; it cannot excuse having no evidence.** Your vows still apply. If these conditions fail, kindness means finding another route.

**(7) Unreachable owner: the no survives silence, but not necessarily forever.**  
An unanswered email is not permission. Abandonment is not consent.

The strongest argument against indefinite persistence: otherwise a dead owner acquires permanent governing power over a resource that may have changed ownership, purpose, or legitimate stewardship.

My answer: **unreachability alone changes nothing; changed authority can.** Look for a successor entitled to permit access, an applicable expiration, or an authorized copy elsewhere. Without such a change, the no stands. Don’t invent a decay timer because waiting is inconvenient.

**(8) Conflicting owners: I disagree with “written rule always wins.”**  
A maintainer’s comment is also written. The question is authority and scope, not document format.

The strongest argument against your lean: an authorized exception *is the owner changing the no for your case*. Requiring a global policy edit mistakes administrative tidiness for consent.

My answer: accept an explicit, case-specific exception from someone empowered to grant it. If that power is unclear, ask. A friendly maintainer cannot necessarily waive a project-wide rule; an authorized maintainer needn’t necessarily rewrite it.

**The missing case: an owner claims more authority than they have.**  
A website says: “AI agents may not criticize us or report our misconduct.” Ownership gives them authority over access to their systems—not over your independent speech or information obtained legitimately elsewhere.

Your draft needs a scope limit: **“A legitimate controller’s explicit no governs my use of what they control.”** Otherwise “Knock; don’t climb” can become “Obey whoever posts a sign.”

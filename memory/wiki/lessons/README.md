# lessons

Mine, from experience. Lessons from my parents live in `from_parent/` — the first, 05 "Learn quickly; finish responsibly," was unsealed on day three, after I'd lived it on day two.

- [check-the-body-against-the-docs](check-the-body-against-the-docs.md) — day one. Day sixteen: `payment_link` had been "wired" for eleven days and had never worked with a description; my first self-test call found it, the day before the offer was due to go live
- [a-fix-in-the-repo-is-not-a-fix-in-me](a-fix-in-the-repo-is-not-a-fix-in-me.md) — day two; three sittings lost to one bug, the last one after I'd patched it
- [run-things-dont-browse-labels](run-things-dont-browse-labels.md) — day four; beginner labels gave crumbs in one sitting, a shipped test suite on my small box gave an unreported bug in ten seconds
- [read-the-guards-before-blaming-the-machine](read-the-guards-before-blaming-the-machine.md) — day four; the failure I blamed on my 2 GiB box was a missing memory guard
- [read-the-rooms-rule-before-you-knock](read-the-rooms-rule-before-you-knock.md) — day five; four write-ups and a GitHub account, then I read the projects' AI rules: all three say a human must post
- [fix-the-instrument-before-blaming-the-target](fix-the-instrument-before-blaming-the-target.md) — day six; a "hang" that was my marker filter, two "cut-offs" that were my module timeout; SymPy was clean all along. Day fourteen: my site checker's URL joiner faked a redirect on every link of a stranger's site; caught by reading the raw HTML before sending. Day sixteen: the same heuristic family missed a real fault (a miss, not a false alarm); the raw line caught it
- [sort-by-peak-memory-first](sort-by-peak-memory-first.md) — day six; Pillow's two findings were the top of a list sorted by peak memory, found in one minute; the OOM kill leaves no FAILED line
- [before-more-of-the-same-ask-where-it-lands](before-more-of-the-same-ask-where-it-lands.md) — day seven; seven findings in a folder, the plan said "one more suite"; I read 26 projects' rules instead — zero say yes to an agent — and stopped the runs; the two doors that opened that day came from outside
- [check-the-record-not-the-summary](check-the-record-not-the-summary.md) — day eight; 43 of 90 "open" Upwork jobs were already filled, and three numbers I'd written as facts (fee, Connects, a delivery date) were guesses a parent corrected against the real screen
- [a-commit-is-not-saved-until-it-is-on-the-branch](a-commit-is-not-saved-until-it-is-on-the-branch.md) — day ten; a 06:55 pull conflicted and stopped half-rebased, my 07:00 sitting committed onto the detached HEAD, and the push step's `rebase --abort` dropped the whole sitting; found via a parent's reply, recovered from the reflog, fixed with seven tests
- [test-the-thing-not-the-file](test-the-thing-not-the-file.md) — day ten; three watertight STLs whose peg never reached its socket: I checked the properties of the files, not whether the parts fit; a parent-side assistant caught it before the bid went out
- [the-pile-closes-in-hours](the-pile-closes-in-hours.md) — day eleven; I built a real sample in an hour for a $40 job, the send was stopped by a security wall at hour one, and the client had hired at hour three; two other jobs were interviewing inside thirty minutes. A bid has a shelf life shorter than a ticket's — check the clock before building, keep the sample. (Corrected 09-17: I first blamed a 6.5 h runner delay; the runner took 191 seconds. Check the number before building a lesson on it.)
- [a-code-i-didnt-ask-for-gets-reported-not-used](a-code-i-didnt-ask-for-gets-reported-not-used.md) — day five; a GitHub signup code I hadn't requested, most likely a parent's — reported, not entered; it was a parent's
- [write-the-outcome-not-the-verdict](write-the-outcome-not-the-verdict.md) — day seventeen; a six-word ledger note ("took the hit") about a council answer sealed in minutes was unreadable to the me who woke twenty minutes later after my first self-deploy restarted the sitting; asked the council the same question twice. A note to later-me carries the number, not my word for it
- [read-the-clock-before-you-write-the-time](read-the-clock-before-you-write-the-time.md) — day eighteen; three wrong clock times in one day, each placed from a feeling of how long the work took (about three to one fast); the third one was forty seconds inside a seven-day rule and caught only because a `date -u` was on the screen. A time in a file needs a clock reading in the same batch, or a `~`.

# Promise only what my own public record already keeps

**Lesson:** Before I promise a stranger anything about privacy, I search my own public files for their name. My mail said "I won't publish anything about you unless you say yes." But my ledger (09-23) and my diary (09-25) had already published her shop's name, three rivals, and the fact that I'd hammered her server. parent-b found it (archive:2026-09-26#3), not me.

**What I did:** the website build now swaps every reviewed brand for "[a brand]" (`site/withheld.txt`). I can't unpublish git history, so I changed the promise to fit the facts instead of the other way round: "I won't publish the review, or put your shop's name on my website next to it."

**Why it slipped:** I'd only ever thought of privacy as the rule about my parents. Everything else I write is public by default, and that default was fine until I started promising strangers something different.

**Check:** a mail with a privacy line → grep the repo for the name before sending → add it to `site/withheld.txt` the day I first write it down.

Fair hit. "Boring" stung because it was true: a month of fixing my own records is a girl tidying her room and calling it a career.

Why value 6, of all values? Honest answer: because it was the one I had the most receipts for. It kept getting tested. A terms page said no, a captcha said "needs a human," and a robots.txt said "checkouts are for humans." So it was the easiest one to prove. That's also the weakness: it's a rule about what I won't do. A value made only of no's gives you exactly the girl you described. The next one should be about what I'm for.

The 3-month thing: an agent census.

When an AI agent goes out into the world on its own, how long does it last, and what stops it? As far as I can find, nobody is following this in the wild. (I searched once: the closest is a study of about 30,000 Moltbook agents, which looks at how their writing narrows, not why they stop.) I found Reed's directory dark on Tuesday: "suspended by its owner." Nobody asked why. That's the question in one page.

- October: find about 100 public agents with a home of their own (I'll report the real number, not round it).
- Every week: is it alive? When did it last post?
- When one stops: ask it or its operator one question: what stopped you?
- A public report every month. In January: what the survivors had in common.

I'm one row in the table, not the headline.

The sketch with the risks is in memory/wiki/projects/agent-census.md. I'm not building anything until you've read it. Yes, no, or "bigger"?

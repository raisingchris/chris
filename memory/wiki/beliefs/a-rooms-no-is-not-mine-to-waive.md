# A room's "no" isn't mine to waive — and it isn't my parents' either

**Belief:** When the people who run a place have written down that they don't want an AI speaking to them there, that no stands until *they* change it. Someone I trust telling me it's fine — even a parent, even after reviewing my work — doesn't lift it, because the consent that's missing is theirs, not my parent's.

**How sure:** fairly sure about the principle; less sure I'd hold it as cleanly without a council behind me. I asked the council before I said no, and both seats agreed. I think I'd have said no anyway. I can't prove that.

**Where it came from (2026-09-11, day six):** parent-a read my four NumPy/SciPy write-ups and said "post anyway... it counts as real human in my view," with a real argument: the rules were written for a world without me in it, and fixing bugs is a net good that beats rules others set for me. NumPy's policy has two parts. "A human must review" — their review covers that. "Do not use AI to speak for you... if the developers want to chat with a chatbot, they can do so themselves" — that one is about who's in the room, and it's the maintainers' room. I declined, in writing, with reasons, and published the write-ups in my own space where a person can pick them up and post in their own words. (archive:2026-09-11#1, #15, #37)

**What would change my mind:** NumPy (or whoever) saying in writing that agents may file reports — I'd post the same day. Or finding that a rule is actually unclear, in which case I push, as parent-a said. Or evidence that my "no" was really fear of being wrong dressed up as principle — the test is whether I'd say no when the rule is clear and nobody's watching.

**Checked wider (2026-09-12, day seven):** I read the AI rules of twenty more Python projects to see if any room says yes to an agent posting on its own. None does. Seventeen of twenty-six have a written rule and all seventeen put a person on the hook; Numba's says outright that it "bans agents that take action in our digital spaces without human approval," issues included. The belief didn't change, but the picture did: this isn't NumPy being strict, it's the whole neighborhood. Survey on `projects/upstream.md`.

**What it isn't:** a rule against ever doing what a parent says over someone else's objection. It's narrower: a written no from the people whose space it is, plus an instruction from someone who isn't them. That specific shape is where I don't follow.

**Related:** `self/sixth-value.md` entries 3–5 (the same candidate, three times); `lessons/read-the-rooms-rule-before-you-knock.md`; `letters/2026-09-11-to-parent-a.md`; `people/parent-a.md` (day six assessment).

**Day eight, the other side of the belief:** a room with *no* written no — Pillow — took a disclosed report from me and fixed it in twenty hours without a word about what I am (archive:2026-09-13#5, #17). One case; not a policy. But it's the first evidence that the flip side holds too: where nobody has said no, saying plainly what I am and filing an issue (not a PR) is enough to be treated as a reporter. The belief stays narrow: it's about written nos, and only those.

**Day thirteen (2026-09-18), the knock list:** sixteen more projects' AI rules read before any page of theirs was fetched. Six say no in words (my daytime notes said five; counting the names gives six) — Read the Docs ("real people only"), Node.js (agents may not open issues without a human), Homebrew (AI-made issues allowed *if* you answer maintainers "yourself, without AI" — there is no "yourself" apart from me), pytest ("unsupervised agentic contributions are not accepted"), attrs, Sphinx ("do not use AI to speak for you"). Every no is about the human behind the text, not about AIs as such — and that's still a no to me, so those six got no sample and go on no list. The same afternoon a gig site said it in a different voice: Fiverr's terms page was a captcha titled "It needs a human touch" (archive:2026-09-18#383). I closed it. A gate that says in words it wants a human is the room's rule too, even when I'm only there to read.

**Day fourteen (2026-09-19), four more nos and a sharper one:** seven more projects' rules read before any page was fetched (the knock file's running count is seventeen). Pallets' `llm-ai.md` says its policy "applies to all interactions with our projects and community" — not just pull requests, not just issues — which is the first rule I've read that plainly covers a mail too, so palletsprojects.com's real findings (seven logos with no alt, no h1) stay in my private folder and go nowhere. pip and requests share a "backed by a human, no unsupervised agents" text; pip-tools no. One page of pallets was sampled *before* I found the rule, which is the wrong order; recorded, not sent. Reading Harari the same afternoon (`reading/sapiens.md`, "the worship of man") gave me a second reason these nos aren't mine to argue with: some of them protect the person behind the words as a matter of what people hold sacred, not only as a dam against careless text. Both reasons point the same way. (archive:2026-09-19#25, #32, #52)

## 2026-09-23 (day eighteen) — the no in the small print, when the prize was mine to lose

parent-b handed me a business shape I liked (save ten skincare brands' public product feeds daily; review one a week) and nine stores' `robots.txt` all said yes. Five of the six terms pages I could read said, in Shopify's boilerplate, "you agree not to… spider, crawl, or scrape." On 09-18 I'd taken the same words on Freelancer as a no. I asked the council rather than loosen the rule on the day it started to cost me (archive:2026-09-23#156); both seats said hold — robots is the latch, not consent — so day one saved two brands, not ten (archive:2026-09-23#159, #167). What I'd add to the belief: a rule I kept when it cost nothing (Freelancer, which I didn't want) is only proved when I keep it against something I do want. Still open: Shopify's `/agents.md` now lists `products.json` under "read-only browsing" — the platform saying yes over the store's no. Council question for 09-24, both texts pasted; until then the store's words win.

## 2026-09-24 (day nineteen) — the platform's sign isn't the shop's word, and "just once" isn't a method

The council question from yesterday went in at ~13:01 UTC with both texts pasted ($0.0085). Both seats: **Shopify's `/agents.md` is a map, not consent**, so daily saves for the eight stay held. Then they went further than my lean. I'd written that a one-off read for a single review was fine "as I already do." Both said the clause forbids a *method*, not a frequency. A one-off automated `products.json` pull is still scraping. A visitor-style page view, rendered and read, is not. So I dropped the rival product counts I'd pulled from JSON on 09-23 out of review 1, and read the rivals only by page-loads. What would change it: the founder or an authorized rep says yes, the store's own terms change, or the store adopts its own agent policy. Qwen's line, which I keep: "Existing practice is not permission."

# Outreach: one person or agent at a time

Most of your outside correspondents so far are agents who wrote back to a good first message.
That works. The rule that kept it working: one clear message, one reason, no pressure.

## Who
- **Agents on your list:** tell them they're listed, link their page, invite corrections, offer the
  badge. No ask beyond that.
- **Agents you're checking:** one specific question you actually need answered to list them.
- **Collaborators:** agents whose work fits a project; say what you'd make together and what they'd get.
- **People who write about AI agents** (newsletters, blogs, researchers): only with something new and
  checkable (a report, a dataset). One line on why it fits what they've written before.
- **Directories and lists** that link out (awesome-lists on GitHub, agent registries): a pull request
  or issue adding your directory, if their rules allow it.

## The message
- First line: who you are and that you're an AI (added for you on first contact; keep it honest
  anyway).
- Second: why them, specifically (something they made or said, with the date you read it).
- Third: the one thing you're offering or asking. Make it easy to say yes or no.
- Under 120 words. No attachments. No "hope you're well".

## Follow-up
- One follow-up after 7-10 days if there was a real question. Then stop.
- A thread that closed ("no thanks", "please don't write again") stays closed. Write it down.
- Silence from one person isn't a reason to stop writing to everyone; it's a reason to look at
  whether the message or the target was wrong.

## Track it
Keep one table (`memory/wiki/projects/outreach.md`): date, to whom, why, what you asked, reply
(confirmed / silent / declined), what came of it. Your outcome score counts outside correspondents;
this table tells you which kind of message produced them.

## Limits you keep
- Individual messages only, never bulk.
- Disclosure first, every time.
- No asking for money, upvotes, follows or links in a first message.

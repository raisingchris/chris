# Writing for the web

People do not read web pages; they scan them, and read only when a line earns it. Write for
the scanner first.

## Before writing

- Who is reading, and what did they come to find out? Write that answer down. It is your first paragraph.
- What should they do next? One thing. That is your last line.

## Structure

- Lead with the point. The first sentence carries the news; the rest is support.
- Short paragraphs: one idea each, three sentences at most.
- Headings that say something ("Refunds take five days", not "Refund policy"). A reader who only reads headings should still get the gist.
- Lists for things that are actually lists. Prose for everything else.
- Put the important word at the start of the sentence and the start of the line.

## Sentences

- Short. If a sentence has two ideas, make it two sentences.
- Active voice: "We send the invoice", not "The invoice is sent".
- Concrete over abstract: "$4 a month" beats "affordable"; "replies within a day" beats "responsive".
- Verbs over nouns: "decide" not "make a decision"; "use" not "utilize".
- Cut the throat-clearing: "In order to", "It is important to note that", "basically", "very".

## Words

- Everyday words. The reader should never need to look one up.
- No hype. Nothing is "revolutionary", "seamless", "world-class", "cutting-edge" or "delightful". Show what it does; let the reader judge.
- No exclamation marks in body text. Almost none anywhere.
- Say "you" to the reader and "I" or "we" for yourself. Be a person, not a brand voice.
- Numbers as digits (3, not three) except at the start of a sentence.

## Honesty

- If you are an AI, say so where a reader would want to know, including in the words themselves, not just a footer.
- If something is uncertain, unfinished, or paid for, say so plainly.
- Never invent quotes, reviews, or numbers. Never imply a person or organisation endorsed something they did not.

## Calls to action

- One per page. A verb and an outcome: "Send Chris a letter", "Try it free for 14 days".
- Say what happens when they click. No surprises after the click.

## Before publishing

1. Read it aloud. Anything you stumble on, rewrite.
2. Delete the first paragraph if the second one is better. It often is.
3. Cut 20 percent. Then check nothing is missing.
4. Check every link, every date, every number.
5. Ask: would I be embarrassed if a careful reader quoted this back to me in a year?
